Good morning, listeners. Today is September 20, 2026. Overnight, the S&P 500 closed up 12.74 points, or 0.17 percent, after dipping to 7610 intraday. The Nasdaq added 104 points, gaining 0.39 percent. Volume spiked on Triple Witching Day, the third Friday of September. Stock options, futures, and index options all expired simultaneously. That inflates volume but does not shift the underlying trend.

The real story this week was the Fed. The central bank hiked rates, and the hawkish tone triggered a sharp selloff on Wednesday. But here is the critical nuance. This was the smallest rate increase in roughly fifty years. The dot plot shows one more hike this year and at most one next year. That is a cumulative seventy-five basis points. The policy toolbox is running thin.

Every rate-hike cycle since the 1980s has produced a positive S&P return. The market is not in a 2022-style bear setup. Ten-year Treasury yields have broken above five percent. August bond inflows hit a record high. The allocation math is simple. Lock in a five percent yield for a decade. That is compelling.

But the bears are not silent. Bank of America strategists are flagging a path to five percent on the two-year. Rate swaps are pricing three more hikes into the 4.50 to 4.75 range. BofA is bearish on the two-year, expecting the yield to push through five. The divergence is real. The consensus says tightening is nearly over. The risk case says it is not.

After two days of digestion, the hawkish overhang has largely cleared. The market has absorbed the tightening shock. The dominant narrative is rotating back to artificial intelligence.

On the geopolitical front, the US and China are expected to hold a summit in Washington next week. Trump is reportedly planning to meet the Chinese president at the airport. That breaks standard American diplomatic protocol. The signal is deliberate. A trade truce extension past October is the headline prize. A one-year extension, or even a full-term deal, would be a major positive for equities. AI safety coordination between the two powers is a secondary but meaningful outcome. The core risk is not a new tariff. The core risk is a sudden, unilateral escalation. As long as that does not happen, the macro floor holds.

Now let us connect that liquidity picture to the tech complex. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index gapped down early this week on AI safety concerns. A coalition of nearly a hundred researchers from Stanford, Johns Hopkins, and nonprofit labs published an open letter. They are demanding third-party safety audits with deep access to frontier model internals. That scare hit the semi tape hard.

But by Friday, the gap was fully filled. Twenty-six of thirty components closed higher. Only four were red. The index has effectively bottomed and is entering a rebound. The pure memory and storage names are leading the recovery. When that segment turns, the broader index finds its next leg up.

Capital is rotating with conviction. Berkshire Hathaway completed its leadership transition this week. Warren Buffett stepped down as chairman. His son Howard is now non-executive chairman, focused on agriculture and food security research. But the real operator is Greg Abel. He holds full operational and capital allocation authority over a multi-hundred-billion-dollar book. And Abel has started deploying into AI. He is buying Google, both in the open market and through a private placement. That is a signal. The largest value investor in the world is tilting toward the AI capex cycle.

The HBM supply chain is the tightest link right now. Long-term supply agreements have locked in pricing for the next two years. Any capacity expansion converts directly into revenue and margin expansion. That is why the memory names are the most active in the entire market.

Let us go deep on the three names that matter most today.

Micron Technology. Earnings land at the end of the month. The street is modeling earnings per share up roughly ten times year over year. That is not a typo. Ten times. The HBM contract book is fully allocated. Long-term agreements have fixed the price floor. Capacity additions flow straight to the bottom line. The stock has reclaimed the thousand-dollar level. And here is the key point. The valuation has not stretched. A ten-fold earnings expansion has fully absorbed the price gain. Micron is the single most traded name in the market by dollar volume. Institutional flow is concentrated and aggressive. The risk is cyclical. Memory pricing is not a straight line. A future price correction could pull the stock back toward its starting point. But right now, this is the strongest momentum name in semis. Support sits just below the thousand-dollar mark. A close below that invalidates the short-term thesis.

Netflix. Wells Fargo just cut the rating to Underweight. The target price dropped from eighty dollars to fifty-seven. That is a twenty-eight percent haircut. The logic is structural, not cyclical. Viewing hours are declining as users fragment across platforms. User growth hit a ceiling roughly two years ago, and the company stopped reporting the metric. There is no connection to the AI trade. AI-generated short-form content is actually cannibalizing screen time. The hit franchises are wrapped. Stranger Things is over. Squid Game is over. The pipeline is thin. If a streamer cannot sustain high growth, the market reclassifies it as a utility. And Netflix does not pay a utility-grade dividend. The capital has a better home. The streaming crown remains intact. But the growth premium is being stripped away. Any bounce from here is a mean-reversion trade, not a trend trade.

Tesla. No major catalyst in the near term. The stock is grinding higher in a slow, orderly uptrend. The gap from the last earnings print has been fully filled. Without a new product announcement or a policy tailwind, Tesla will simply track the broader index. There is no independent alpha here right now. It is a hold, not a chase.

One final note on positioning. Qualcomm is the outlier. It fell more than five percent on Friday, directly against the sector. The Philadelphia Semi Index gapped up. Qualcomm gapped down. That divergence is a warning. If the semi rebound continues, Qualcomm either catches up or it is being sold for a reason. Watch the next two sessions closely.
