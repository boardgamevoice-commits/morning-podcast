Good morning, listeners. Today is November 03, 2026.

Overnight, the Dow rose against the tide. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq gave up early gains and closed lower.
The Nasdaq fell 1.07 percent to 25,164. The S&P 500 lost 0.51 percent, or about 39 points.
The S&P has now closed below its half-year moving average for two straight sessions.
Money kept rotating out of technology and into insurance, healthcare, credit and internet retail.
The ten-year Treasury yield slid back below 5 percent. Some money leaving the AI trade went into bonds.
Oil fell along with yields. But the VIX climbed as high as 21.7, and that ended the intraday rebound.

Iran drove the drop in oil.
Before the open, Tehran offered a phased plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within days.
Secretary of State Rubio said President Trump prefers diplomacy and is holding off on new strikes.
By the afternoon, Iranian media said messages had passed through intermediaries, with no progress.
The talks have not broken down, so crude held its losses. But Iran's core demands have not changed.
The risk is that strikes resume after the midterms. If they do, oil could jump again.

The midterm election is today, and results are expected tonight.
Prediction markets give Democrats a 94 percent chance of taking the House. They give 75 percent for the Senate.
Bulls say divided government brings policy stability. Bears warn that tax cuts and deregulation could be reversed.
History favors the bulls. The S&P 500 has risen in the twelve months after every midterm since 1950.
The average gain has been roughly 14 to 15 percent.
Still, the election is a secondary driver. Monetary policy and earnings decide the direction.

On the Fed, the debate is about rate hikes, not cuts.
CME futures put the odds of a December rate hike at 72 percent.
The economy is still running warm. The ISM manufacturing index rose to 55.5.
That is ten straight months of expansion.
This week's jobs data matters a lot. The ADP report comes Wednesday, with 65,000 to 85,000 jobs expected.
Friday's payrolls are expected near 70,000, with unemployment steady at 4.2 percent.
With the labor market near full employment, inflation will decide what happens in December.

The Fear and Greed Index sits at 23, which is extreme fear.
That is not a capitulation signal yet. Past bottoms reached 3 on Liberation Day and 6 during the Iran war.
Q-Q-Q failed at 700 and now sits near its 250-day moving average.
If that line breaks, the next level to watch is near 660.
On the Nasdaq Composite, watch 25,000 and the annual line near 24,700.
For the S&P 500, the bearish downside target is near 7,200.
In the past, breaks of the annual line have paid off for patient buyers. But history does not repeat on schedule.

The selling is centered on AI hardware.
The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 3.4 percent to 10,328.
Just below sits 10,000. The annual line, a round number and a Fibonacci level all meet there.
A bounce from that zone would not be surprising. Until chips stabilize, the broader correction is unlikely to end.
Demand is not the problem. South Korea's October chip exports jumped 259 percent to 56.4 billion dollars.
Even so, foreign investors kept selling Korean chip names like SK Hynix.

The real worry is the cost of the AI buildout.
Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Oracle cut buybacks by 83 percent in the first three quarters.
That is down from 81.8 billion to 13.6 billion dollars. It is the lowest in at least fifteen years.
Instead, they are borrowing. Alphabet plans a six billion euro bond.
Meta is weighing at least 20 billion dollars in debt across the dollar and euro markets.
Off-balance-sheet commitments at the hyperscalers, Nvidia, Broadcom and SpaceX now total 4.11 trillion dollars.
People owned the megacaps as cash machines. That case is weakening, and investors are losing patience.
On the other side, White House adviser Kevin Hassett says AI funding remains plentiful.
He sees no risk of a broad selloff.
Bill Ackman is buying the dip in Amazon, Meta and Microsoft.

The biggest deal shock came in optical networking.
Marvell agreed to buy Lumentum in an all-stock deal, at 4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share.
Lumentum holders will own 33 percent of the combined company. The deal is expected to close in early 2028.
There is no cash premium. Lumentum fell 18.6 percent.
Marvell fell 12.7 percent and broke below its annual line.
The strategy makes sense. Marvell needs Lumentum's indium phosphide lasers to complete its optical platform.
Management expects more than 1.1 billion dollars a year in cost savings.
But two richly valued stocks are being marked down together, and Chinese approval is uncertain.
Lumentum's revenue rose 136 percent, and even that could not stop the slide.

Palantir beat on every major line. Revenue jumped 96 percent to 2.31 billion dollars.
For the first time, U.S. commercial revenue was larger than government revenue.
Fourth quarter guidance came in above forecasts. Still, the stock slipped after hours on weak short-term momentum.

Four stocks have clear signals today: two buys and two sells.

Nvidia is the first buy.
Among the five leading chip stocks, it has the largest market cap.
It also has the lowest valuation and the strongest growth.
At nearly five trillion dollars, it will not rise tenfold. But its moat makes it steadier than most.
Buy around 203 dollars, near the current price.
Add if it breaks below 199 dollar support.
Your risk gauge is the chip index holding 10,000.

Berkshire Hathaway B shares are the defensive buy, but only on a pullback.
They hit a record high in October, while many stocks nearly halved.
They also rose during the 2022 bear market.
Cash and Treasury bills totaled 365.5 billion dollars at the end of the second quarter.
Over five years, the stock returned about 92 percent, beating all three major indexes.
Do not chase the record. Start buying near 530 dollar support.
Add near 512 dollars. This is a position to build steadily and hold for the long term.

Apple is a take-profits signal.
Fiscal fourth quarter revenue reached about 114.5 billion dollars, up 12 percent.
Earnings per share were 2.10 dollars, above the 2.00 dollar estimate.
iPhone and Mac sales both beat.
But five cents of that came from tariff refunds.
Without them, gross margin was about 46.9 percent, below expectations.
Greater China revenue missed, and iPad and wearables also disappointed.
The stock fell about 3 percent after hours.
Investors seeking safety pushed Apple up about 25 percent from its June low.
It now trades near 39 times forward earnings, roughly double the S&P 500.
The average analyst target is 332 dollars, below the recent price near 338.
Sell into any bounce back toward 338 dollars.
If Apple keeps sliding, the market loses one of its usual stabilizers.

Oracle is the sell.
Five days after its investor day, Oracle broke its own financing promise.
It had pledged no more than 20 billion dollars in new funding through fiscal 2030.
Now it is raising the full 20 billion at once, half in stock and half in bonds.
Long-term debt is near 117 billion dollars.
Free cash flow is expected at negative 23.7 billion dollars this year.
S&P Global Ratings already rates Oracle B-B-B minus, one notch above junk.
Another downgrade would force investment-grade funds to sell.
Credit default swap prices have spiked several times.
Key support is 114 dollars. A break there opens a path toward 100 dollars.
Use rallies to cut exposure. At best, Oracle is a short-term trade, not a long-term hold.

Keep cash on hand. Buy at your levels, not on headlines.
